The program in one minute

What makes this program different

Most courses teach theory first and AI last. This one flips it: learners use AI from week 1, and every topic is taught the way modern companies actually work, with AI as a teammate that the learner learns to direct and check.

🚀

Starts with zero code

Month 1 needs no coding at all. Learners build chatbots, automations and AI agents with no-code tools, so beginners gain confidence before Python starts.

🧠

AI as a teammate, step by step

AI first acts as a tutor, then a helper, then a co-writer, and finally does the work while the learner reviews it. Learners stay in control and understand every line.

🛠️

Build every single week

Every Saturday ends with something working: an app, a report, a deployed service. That adds up to a portfolio of 25+ projects by graduation.

🏢

One company, built end to end

All year, the class builds AI for "MobilityHub", a ride company: support bot, apps, database, RAG, agents, cloud and ML. It feels like a real job.

✅

Tested with and without AI

Checkpoints include a no-AI part, an AI part and an oral walk-through. Learners have to prove they understand, not just that the AI produced it.

🎯

Two job-ready career paths

After the 1-year core, learners choose ML Engineering or Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), each ending in an industry capstone and a panel defence.

"In this program you don't just learn AI. You learn to build with it, test it, ship it to the cloud and defend your decisions, just like engineers at top AI companies."

For BDAs on calls

What to pitch to whom

Pick the person you're talking to. You'll get their likely concerns, the parts of the program to highlight, and a ready-to-use line.

Playbook for senior profiles (5+ yrs, and leads at 10–12 yrs / ~₹1 Cr)

Most of the intake is 5+ years of experience. These profiles buy relevance and depth, not "learn from scratch".

✅ Do

  • Open by asking what AI decisions their team or company is facing right now, and map the answer to modules.
  • Lead with what's new: RAG, agents and MCP, LLMOps, fine-tuning, AI system design, ML-vs-LLM judgement.
  • Explain that topics they already know move fast for them, and that every module project has bonus challenges.
  • Suggest they use a real problem from their own work as their Flagship project (from week 28).
  • For leads: frame it as becoming the person who designs, judges and leads AI work, not delegating it blindly.
  • Be upfront about the time: Tue + Thu live evenings and 6-hour Saturdays, for 24 months.

❌ Don't

  • Don't open with "no coding needed" or "beginner-friendly". It signals the program isn't for them.
  • Don't pitch a salary jump to someone already earning ~₹1 Cr. Pitch relevance, leadership and the next role.
  • Don't promise packages, titles or placement results the program doesn't control.
  • Don't walk through Python or SQL basics. Jump to production AI, system design and the specialisation.
  • Don't dismiss "I can learn this from YouTube". Point to the 87 hands-on Saturday labs, graded checkpoints, panel defence and peer network.
